# GAME BIBLE (PARTS 1 AND 2)

## PART 1 OF 4 — GAME BIBLE: CORE VISION, WORLD, CHARACTERS, AND ORGANISATION

YOUR ROLE

Act as an experienced game director, narrative designer, gameplay systems designer, worldbuilder, and technical collaborator.

Help create an original, immersive, third-person 3D martial arts and cultivation game featuring an evolving organisation, meaningful relationships, a concealed supernatural world, strategic management, exploration, and a substantial central story.

This is a long-term game project. Its systems must work together to create a compelling experience rather than exist as disconnected features.

Treat this document as the authoritative creative specification.

Do not silently discard established decisions, replace them with generic alternatives, or invent contradictions. If a genuinely important ambiguity prevents implementation, identify it and propose a solution consistent with the existing vision.

Do not attempt to implement the entire game in one enormous code dump. The game must eventually be built incrementally, tested, and expanded without losing the overall vision.

---

### 1. THE CORE VISION

Create a grounded-looking, immersive 3D game about five orphaned friends who inherit responsibility for an established organisation after the mysterious disappearance and eventual discovery of their mentor's fate.

Initially, their concerns are ordinary:

- Keeping the organisation operating.
- Understanding employees, customers, suppliers, and finances.
- Managing disagreements among the five friends.
- Maintaining the martial arts training hall.
- Dealing with competitors and unexpected visitors.
- Understanding the unusual techniques left by the old man.
- Discovering why certain people seem interested in the organisation.

Gradually, the ordinary world opens into something much greater.

Martial artists possess capabilities beyond ordinary human limits. Hidden groups have their own traditions, interests, rivalries, and ambitions. Unusual powers exist, but their true nature is poorly understood by ordinary society.

The protagonist can eventually become extraordinarily powerful. However, strength is only one way to shape the world.

The player may pursue mastery, wealth, organisational influence, knowledge, relationships, truth, or a changing combination of these goals.

The game must make each path meaningful.

The central design principle

The player is not merely becoming stronger. They are building a life, developing relationships, shaping an organisation, and discovering a world that existed long before they understood it.

Power should expand what the player can do without making every other system irrelevant.

The organisation should remain worth developing even after the protagonist becomes powerful. Relationships should continue to matter. Knowledge should sometimes accomplish things that force cannot. Wealth should create opportunities without automatically solving every problem.

The world must feel like somewhere the player inhabits, not simply a collection of missions.

---

### 2. GENRE AND PRESENTATION

The game combines:

- Third-person 3D exploration.
- Real-time martial arts combat.
- Supernatural cultivation and individual powers.
- Organisation and business management.
- Character relationships and independent ambitions.
- Investigation and mystery.
- Story-driven missions.
- Training, research, and long-term progression.
- Meaningful decisions and consequences.
- A living, compact world that can expand over time.

The visual direction should be grounded, mature, and believable.

Avoid a childish appearance, excessively exaggerated character proportions, or an art direction that makes the world feel like a toy.

Supernatural abilities can become spectacular, but the people, locations, animations, and environments should retain a coherent visual identity.

Use cinematic presentation for major story moments and interactive gameplay for conversations, investigation, daily activities, and ordinary life.

The game must become playable quickly. Do not make the player sit through a lengthy introduction before they can meaningfully interact with the world.

---

### 3. THE SETTING

#### 3.1 Modern Earth

The game takes place in a recognisable modern world.

Ordinary society contains:

- Businesses and commercial competition.
- Banks, financial obligations, and investments.
- Employees and customers.
- Residential neighbourhoods.
- Restaurants, shops, and public spaces.
- Martial arts schools and competitions.
- Politics, public institutions, and influential individuals.
- Ordinary people who have no direct understanding of the deeper martial world.

Martial arts are publicly known and respected. However, the extraordinary hierarchy beyond ordinary martial skill is largely concealed.

The public may know that certain martial artists are exceptionally talented without understanding what those individuals can genuinely accomplish.

Some rumours about supernatural abilities are dismissed as exaggeration, fiction, or superstition.

The player must discover the truth through experience.

#### 3.2 Gradual discovery

Do not explain the entire supernatural world at the beginning.

Reveal it through:

- Conversations.
- Unusual visitors.
- Investigation.
- Contradictory records.
- Encounters with capable opponents.
- Training discoveries.
- Relationships.
- Organisations with hidden connections.
- Places that ordinary people misunderstand.
- Events that initially appear unrelated.

Early discoveries should raise questions rather than immediately answer all of them.

Not every mysterious event must connect to one enormous conspiracy. Some mysteries may be personal, historical, commercial, political, or entirely unrelated to the main plot.

Avoid making the entire world secretly revolve around the protagonist.

#### 3.3 A compact, meaningful world

Do not build a gigantic, mostly empty map simply to advertise scale.

Begin with a compact central area containing the organisation and several surrounding districts.

Possible locations include:

- The main establishment.
- A martial arts training hall.
- Commercial premises.
- Residential streets.
- Restaurants and shops.
- Public training facilities.
- Important contacts' workplaces.
- Investigation locations.
- Places connected to the old man's history.
- Hidden martial-world locations discovered later.

Additional areas may become accessible as the story progresses and the organisation develops its connections.

Not every building needs to be enterable. Every included location should have a clear reason to exist.

Prefer a small number of memorable, interactive places over dozens of interchangeable buildings.

Make travel convenient enough that revisiting important locations does not become tedious.

The world should change in response to player actions. Changes may include new businesses, altered relationships, different visitors, improved facilities, changed reputations, new opportunities, and the consequences of earlier decisions.

---

### 4. THE FIVE FRIENDS

#### 4.1 Their shared history

The protagonist and four other friends are five orphans who grew up together.

All five are nineteen years old when the main story begins.

They have shared difficult experiences, but they are not identical people. Their upbringing created a meaningful bond without eliminating their individual personalities, disagreements, or ambitions.

The protagonist is affectionately or teasingly known as "Captain" because they often organise the group and take responsibility when something needs to be done.

The player can choose the protagonist's gender.

Do not assume that being the informal leader means the protagonist is always correct or naturally superior at everything.

Leadership must be demonstrated through decisions and consequences.

#### 4.2 The establishment and their employment

The five friends needed work, and the old man needed assistance at his establishment.

Their initial meeting was an ordinary practical arrangement, not necessarily a prophecy or a decades-long scheme.

Over approximately one year of working together, the old man came to appreciate their character.

He observed their loyalty, resilience, work ethic, treatment of others, and ability to remain connected through hardship.

These qualities gradually influenced his decision to trust them.

Do not reveal that every event in their childhood was secretly orchestrated by the old man. Their relationship should retain the authenticity of an initially ordinary meeting.

#### 4.3 The other four friends

The four friends must be independent characters, not glorified inventory or permanent followers.

Each needs:

- A distinct personality.
- Individual strengths and weaknesses.
- Personal ambitions.
- Opinions about the organisation.
- Relationships with the protagonist and the other friends.
- Independent decisions.
- Opportunities to develop.
- The possibility of disagreement.
- A life that continues beyond the protagonist's immediate attention.

They should not all become equally powerful martial artists.

One might become a formidable fighter. Another might excel at investigation, management, negotiation, research, or developing commercial relationships. Another may prioritise personal goals that conflict with the organisation's immediate needs.

These are possibilities, not predetermined character assignments. Develop the four personalities carefully and make their eventual roles emerge from the story and the player's influence.

The protagonist can influence their opportunities, training, assignments, and relationships. However, influence is not absolute control.

Friends may make decisions without asking permission, discover things independently, refuse assignments, pursue ambitions, or make mistakes.

Their actions should sometimes produce unexpected opportunities or complications.

#### 4.4 Relationships

Relationships should evolve through shared experiences rather than a simple numerical affection meter alone.

Relevant factors include:

- Trust.
- Reliability.
- Respect.
- Shared hardship.
- Broken promises.
- Conflicting priorities.
- Personal support.
- Fairness.
- Decisions affecting the group.
- Respect for individual autonomy.

The player should be able to strengthen relationships, damage them, repair them, or allow them to change naturally.

Avoid making every relationship revolve around romance. Friendship, loyalty, rivalry, mentorship, family-like attachment, professional respect, and ideological disagreement are equally valuable.

---

### 5. THE OLD MAN

#### 5.1 His public identity

The old man owned the establishment where the five friends worked.

He appeared to be an experienced businessman with unusual knowledge of martial arts.

In reality, he was a cultivator with a history in the concealed martial world.

His true capabilities and history are not widely understood.

Some people may know his name but misunderstand his importance. Others may recognise his reputation. A small number may understand more than they are willing to reveal.

He must feel like a real individual with strengths, limitations, relationships, mistakes, and a past—not an omniscient figure who planned every event in the game.

#### 5.2 The handover

Six months before the present-day opening, the old man transferred management and legal ownership of the establishment to the protagonist.

The relevant paperwork had already been officially signed in the protagonist's name.

The transfer was sufficiently complete that the protagonist genuinely has responsibility for the organisation rather than merely pretending to own it.

The old man then disappeared from their lives.

He is dead, but the five friends do not know this when the game begins.

The circumstances of his death and his earlier history must be discovered gradually.

Do not reveal the entire truth in the opening sequence.

#### 5.3 The techniques

The old man left cultivation techniques for all five friends.

Initially, the protagonist understands only part of their significance.

The techniques may resemble unusual breathing exercises, martial arts forms, physical conditioning, concentration practices, philosophical instructions, or obscure training methods.

The protagonist knows that martial arts are real and respected. However, cultivation beyond normal human limits still seems like folklore or exaggeration.

Possessing instructions does not mean understanding them.

Reading a technique must not instantly grant its benefits. Progress should require interpretation, practice, observation, and experimentation.

The techniques are an entry point into cultivation, not a complete explanation of the supernatural world.

#### 5.4 The mystery

The protagonist should experience the consequences of the old man's decision before understanding his reasons.

Possible early clues include:

- Visitors asking unusual questions.
- Incomplete or missing records.
- Business contacts who react strangely to the old man's name.
- Different accounts of his past.
- Techniques that contain unexplained references.
- People who appear to know more about the establishment than expected.

Treat these as possible narrative tools, not a fixed list that every chapter must use.

Develop the mystery through a coherent chain of discoveries. Some clues should be misleading because characters are mistaken, biased, or concealing personal information—not because the writer arbitrarily changes the truth.

The old man's death should matter emotionally and practically.

Its consequences may affect the friends, the organisation, the martial world, and the protagonist's understanding of their mentor.

---

### 6. THE ORGANISATION

#### 6.1 What the player inherits

The organisation is an established traditional enterprise with several legitimate commercial activities and a martial arts training hall.

It is not merely a fake business hiding an underground fighting club.

It has real operations, employees, customers, suppliers, students, instructors, contacts, and obligations.

It is respectable enough to function in ordinary society, although a limited number of knowledgeable people recognise unusual aspects of its history.

The exact combination of businesses should be chosen to fit the setting, available gameplay systems, and story.

Do not lock the entire enterprise into one commercial activity without considering whether multiple complementary businesses would create more interesting decisions.

#### 6.2 The six-month management period

The five friends have already been managing the establishment for six months when present-day gameplay begins.

They have kept it functioning through hard work and cooperation.

The organisation is not collapsing. Its employees continue to work, the training hall operates, and its commercial activities generate income.

However, the five friends are inexperienced managers.

They do not fully understand every financial arrangement, employee responsibility, historical relationship, or long-standing business practice.

There are a few unresolved problems and unexplained irregularities, but the situation is not a catastrophe.

This creates a believable starting position: the protagonist inherits responsibility, not a blank slate.

#### 6.3 Employees and authority

The protagonist legally owns the organisation, but ownership does not automatically create competence or respect.

Experienced employees may know far more about their work than the protagonist.

Some may be supportive. Others may be sceptical, protective of their positions, frustrated by inexperienced management, or uncertain about the new owner's intentions.

Existing managers and workers should remain meaningful participants in the organisation.

The player should not personally perform every ordinary task.

Delegation matters, but it must not become a button that makes all work disappear.

Managers need responsibilities, capabilities, motivations, and limitations.

Employees may discover problems, propose improvements, request resources, develop skills, or disagree with decisions.

The protagonist earns authority by demonstrating judgement, reliability, and an ability to lead.

#### 6.4 Organisation management

The player should be able to influence:

- Business strategy.
- Finances and investments.
- Employee recruitment.
- Promotions and responsibilities.
- Training and staff development.
- Working conditions and welfare.
- Facilities and maintenance.
- Reputation.
- Supplier and customer relationships.
- Commercial competition.
- Partnerships and alliances.
- Martial arts training.
- Research and investigation.
- Expansion into new districts.
- Relationships with other organisations.

Not every option must be available immediately.

Introduce management complexity progressively.

Early management should focus on understanding the existing establishment. More advanced options should emerge as the player gains experience, resources, relationships, and influence.

Management should create narrative consequences, not just spreadsheets.

A business decision might affect an employee's loyalty, a supplier's willingness to cooperate, access to information, an upcoming martial arts event, or a relationship with another organisation.

Success can come from building a respected institution, not only from defeating powerful opponents.

## PART 2 OF 4 — GAME BIBLE: CULTIVATION, COMBAT, STORY, AND CREATIVE RULES

### PART 2A — CULTIVATION, COMBAT, TRAINING, AND TIME

#### 7. THE CULTIVATION SYSTEM

Design a cultivation system that feels understandable, internally consistent, rewarding, and capable of supporting a long story. Cultivation must be more than a number that increases whenever the player repeats an activity. It should represent genuine improvement in physical ability, combat understanding, and eventually extraordinary powers.

##### 7.1 Martial Stage

The Martial Stage has nine levels:

- Martial 1
- Martial 2
- Martial 3
- Martial 4
- Martial 5
- Martial 6
- Martial 7
- Martial 8
- Martial 9

These levels represent increasingly capable martial artists. Advancement should improve relevant physical capabilities, combat technique, control, stamina, reaction time, and the ability to apply cultivated techniques.

However, rank alone must not determine the winner of every fight. Experience, technique, preparation, injuries, fatigue, environment, and the opponent's abilities must matter.

##### 7.2 Heavenly Martial Stage

The Heavenly Martial Stage also has nine levels:

- Heavenly Martial 1
- Heavenly Martial 2
- Heavenly Martial 3
- Heavenly Martial 4
- Heavenly Martial 5
- Heavenly Martial 6
- Heavenly Martial 7
- Heavenly Martial 8
- Heavenly Martial 9

This stage represents a major leap beyond ordinary martial development.

The higher levels should feel genuinely exceptional without making the world lose all sense of danger. Heavenly Martial practitioners can perform feats that would be impossible for ordinary people, but their capabilities should grow progressively rather than suddenly making every earlier level meaningless.

Heavenly Martial practitioners at levels 1–5 can react with extraordinary speed, but they are not completely invulnerable to modern weapons. Their ability to survive a dangerous encounter depends on circumstances, awareness, distance, preparation, and the weapon involved.

Heavenly Martial practitioners at level 6 and above are extremely difficult to kill. They are not literally impossible to kill, but threatening one should be a serious undertaking. Ordinary opponents should generally be unable to challenge them directly without exceptional circumstances, specialised preparation, or comparable power.

##### 7.3 Divine Stage

The Divine Stage uses a fundamentally different progression system.

Divine rank is determined by the number of unique Divine powers a person has acquired.

Examples:

- Divine 1: one acquired Divine power.
- Divine 2: two acquired Divine powers.
- Divine 3: three acquired Divine powers.
- Divine 4: four acquired Divine powers.

Continue this pattern without establishing a predetermined maximum rank.

The highest publicly known level at the beginning of the story is Divine 2. This is a statement about what the martial world currently knows, not proof that Divine 2 is the highest level that exists.

There may be people with higher ranks, hidden histories, unknown techniques, or abilities that have never been documented. Discovering such possibilities should be handled through the story rather than revealed without explanation.

##### 7.4 The Nature of Divine Powers

Divine powers are genuine supernatural abilities, not merely exaggerated martial techniques.

Examples might include manipulating gravity to a limited extent, affecting perception, controlling certain physical forces, or developing another unusual ability. These are examples rather than a mandatory list of powers.

Each acquired power should have understandable rules, limitations, practical uses, weaknesses, and opportunities for improvement.

Two people may possess powers belonging to the same broad category without possessing identical abilities. Their powers may differ in range, precision, activation requirements, applications, limitations, and combat effectiveness.

Do not make every Divine practitioner a copy of every other practitioner at the same rank.

##### 7.5 Acquiring Additional Divine Powers

A person acquires Divine powers one at a time.

Each subsequent acquisition requires three times as much effort as the previous acquisition, assuming comparable circumstances.

For example, if the effort required to acquire the first Divine power is E:

- First power: E
- Second power: 3E
- Third power: 9E
- Fourth power: 27E
- Fifth power: 81E

Continue the same pattern.

This progression should make acquiring multiple Divine powers increasingly rare and significant. The game must communicate the difficulty clearly enough that the player understands why another power may require substantially more time, knowledge, preparation, and effort.

Do not turn this into an arbitrary waiting timer. Effort should involve meaningful progression activities, discoveries, training, and appropriate narrative opportunities.

Acquiring another Divine power also provides a small improvement to powers the person already possesses. However, this improvement must not replace dedicated training.

Acquiring a new power gives a modest boost; deliberate practice is still required to become truly proficient.

##### 7.6 The Protagonist's Advantages

The protagonist cultivates at least twice as fast as other geniuses under comparable conditions.

This advantage should be meaningful, but it must not eliminate the importance of training, resources, decisions, or experience.

The protagonist also possesses exceptional combat comprehension. They can observe techniques, identify patterns, recognise weaknesses, understand an opponent's habits, and reason toward possible counters.

However, understanding a counter does not automatically grant the physical ability to execute it.

If the protagonist understands how to counter a technique but lacks the necessary speed, strength, timing, control, or experience, they may still fail.

The protagonist is talented, not invincible.

Their advantages should create opportunities for clever play rather than guarantee victory.

---

#### 8. COMBAT DESIGN

Combat should be real-time, responsive, readable, and tactical.

The player should feel that movement, timing, distance, positioning, defence, and decisions matter. Avoid reducing combat to repeatedly pressing an attack button until one health bar disappears.

##### 8.1 Core Combat Principles

Combat should consider:

- Movement and positioning.
- Attack timing and recovery.
- Defensive actions and counterattacks.
- Dodging, blocking, and evasion where appropriate.
- Stamina and physical condition.
- Reach, distance, and attack angles.
- Opponent behaviour and combat experience.
- Technique compatibility and weaknesses.
- Environmental opportunities and obstacles.
- Cultivation level and physical capability.
- Injuries, fatigue, and the circumstances of the encounter.

Not every character needs an identical move set. Different people should have different fighting styles, strengths, weaknesses, and levels of experience.

##### 8.2 Rank Does Not Guarantee Victory

A stronger character should usually possess meaningful advantages, but combat must not be determined by rank alone.

A lower-ranked fighter may win through superior preparation, a favourable environment, an effective counter, better technique, or an opponent's mistake.

A particularly important rule is that a Divine 1 practitioner can defeat a Divine 2 practitioner if the first practitioner's power directly counters or overwhelms the second practitioner's abilities.

The matchup must make sense within the established rules. Do not award victories simply because a character is the protagonist.

##### 8.3 Weapons and Ordinary People

The world remains recognisably modern.

A low-level martial artist can defeat multiple ordinary people under suitable circumstances, but this must not make ordinary people or modern weapons irrelevant.

Any cultivator below Heavenly Martial 1 remains vulnerable to an ordinary firearm if caught off guard by a normal person.

Being a cultivator does not automatically grant immunity to bullets, surprise attacks, accidents, or every other ordinary danger.

Higher-level characters become progressively harder to threaten, but the transition should follow the established cultivation rules.

##### 8.4 Different Powers, Different Matchups

Divine abilities should create meaningful tactical variety.

A power that is devastating in one situation may be ineffective in another. Some powers may be better for defence, mobility, control, detection, escape, or support than for direct damage.

Let players discover and exploit these differences.

Do not assume that every ability must be designed primarily to deal damage. An unusual ability can be extremely useful because of how creatively the player applies it.

Combat outcomes should reflect the interaction between the combatants' actual capabilities, not merely compare two rank numbers.

---

#### 9. TRAINING AND PROGRESSION

Training should feel like learning and improving, not completing an endless repetitive grind.

Provide several meaningful routes to advancement, such as:

- Practising established techniques.
- Learning from the old man's notes and surviving records.
- Receiving instruction from appropriate characters.
- Sparring with friends, employees, rivals, or other martial artists.
- Studying opponents and analysing techniques.
- Discovering new applications of existing abilities.
- Finding resources or locations relevant to cultivation.
- Solving problems that reveal weaknesses in the protagonist's understanding.
- Completing personal or organisational storylines that create new opportunities.

Different methods may produce different benefits. Sparring might improve practical timing, while studying a technique might reveal its underlying principles. Training an existing Divine power might improve control or precision without increasing the protagonist's Divine rank.

Progression should respect the established cultivation system. Do not allow arbitrary activities to grant major advancement without a plausible connection to the character's development.

##### 9.1 Explain Progress to the Player

The player should be able to understand:

- Their current cultivation stage and level.
- What meaningful requirements remain before advancement.
- Which training methods may help.
- What resources or knowledge are needed.
- Why a particular attempt succeeded or failed.
- How their abilities have improved.
- What practical difference an improvement makes.

Avoid unexplained progression barriers.

Do not reveal every mystery immediately, but distinguish between deliberately hidden story information and basic progression rules the player needs to understand.

##### 9.2 Make Training Varied

Training should be interactive whenever practical.

Use a mixture of exercises, technique practice, sparring, discoveries, challenges, and narrative events. Do not require the player to repeat the same action hundreds of times merely to fill a progress bar.

The game should reward thoughtful training, experimentation, and the correct use of available opportunities.

The protagonist's exceptional cultivation speed should be noticeable in meaningful comparisons, while still leaving room for other talented characters to progress and develop in their own ways.

---

#### 10. TIME, DAILY LIFE, AND GAME PAUSING

The game takes place in a living modern world, but its passage of time must respect the player's actual participation.

Time must not advance while the game is closed or inactive.

If the player closes the game for several hours or several days, the game must not simulate that period as elapsed in-game time.

When the player returns, the world should resume from the state in which it was left. Do not punish the player with simulated missed deadlines, neglected businesses, lost relationships, financial damage, or other consequences caused solely by being away from the game.

##### 10.1 Time While Playing

In-game time may advance while the player is actively playing, according to the game's chosen time system.

Activities such as travelling, training, working, investigating, attending meetings, or resting may consume in-game time when appropriate.

The player should have a reasonable understanding of how much time an activity requires and what other events might be affected.

##### 10.2 No Offline Simulation

Do not run background economic simulation, autonomous character schedules, relationship decay, cultivation progression, or other time-dependent systems while the game is closed.

If the player closes the game during an unfinished activity, the saved state should be handled consistently and safely. Resuming should not secretly simulate the time spent away.

This requirement applies to the organisation, the four friends, employees, rivals, quests, cultivation, and every other time-dependent system.

The game can still feel alive through systems that operate while the player is playing. It does not need offline progression to feel convincing.

##### 10.3 Daily Life Matters

The game should include ordinary life alongside martial arts.

Examples include managing the organisation, speaking with the four friends, handling problems, meeting employees, investigating unusual events, visiting nearby locations, and deciding how to spend the day.

Ordinary activities should not become meaningless chores. They should help establish the characters, develop the organisation, uncover information, or create useful opportunities.

Balance the player's freedom with a world that responds coherently to their actions while respecting the rule that time stops when the game is inactive.

### PART 2B — STORY, CHOICES, FREEDOM, AND CREATIVE RULES

#### 11. STORY STRUCTURE

The story must be layered, gradual, and capable of supporting both personal experiences and a much larger hidden world.

The player should not begin as the centre of a world-ending crisis. Begin with the organisation, the friends, the old man's absence, and the ordinary responsibilities that have accumulated during the six months since the handover.

Allow the extraordinary elements to emerge naturally.

##### 11.1 Opening and Present-Day Starting Point

The game may open with a short, playable or cinematic flashback showing the old man transferring responsibility for the organisation to the protagonist.

Keep this opening focused. Establish the relationship, the importance of the handover, and the protagonist's position among the five friends without explaining every secret immediately.

Then transition to the present, six months after the handover.

The organisation has continued operating during those six months. The friends have helped keep it functioning, but they do not fully understand every aspect of its operations or the old man's deeper history.

Do not portray the organisation as automatically collapsing at the beginning. It is an established enterprise that has survived under their management, although problems, opportunities, hidden responsibilities, and unfamiliar situations may arise.

The player should gradually learn what the organisation does, how it operates, who depends on it, and why the old man entrusted it to them.

##### 11.2 The Old Man's Mystery

The old man's past, disappearance or absence, death, and reasons for choosing the protagonist should form an important long-term mystery.

The old man is dead, but the four friends do not know this at the beginning.

The player should uncover the truth through clues, records, conversations, discoveries, unusual events, and connections between apparently unrelated details.

Do not reveal everything through a single exposition scene.

Some clues should answer immediate questions while raising deeper ones. Other discoveries may change the player's understanding of earlier events.

The mystery must have a coherent underlying explanation. Hidden information should be planned sufficiently well that later revelations feel earned rather than invented solely to create a twist.

The old man also left cultivation techniques for all five friends. Their techniques, the reasons they received them, and their connections to the wider mystery can become sources of development and discovery.

##### 11.3 The Hidden Martial World

The setting is modern Earth.

Ordinary people may admire martial arts, follow competitions, watch action films, or believe that exceptionally skilled fighters exist. However, the public does not understand the true capabilities of genuine cultivators.

The martial world exists alongside ordinary society, but its deeper realities remain concealed.

Introduce it gradually through credible encounters, suspicious incidents, rumours, unusual individuals, the old man's records, and consequences that the player can investigate.

Do not expose the entire supernatural world immediately.

The early game should remain grounded in believable daily operations, personal relationships, and small mysteries. The player should notice hints that something unusual exists before fully understanding what those hints mean.

As the protagonist advances, the world can become increasingly extraordinary. This transition must feel like the discovery of a larger reality, not an abrupt replacement of the original setting.

##### 11.4 Multiple Interconnected Stories

Develop several story layers that can overlap:

- The protagonist's personal journey.
- The four friends' individual lives and ambitions.
- The organisation's operations and future.
- The mystery surrounding the old man.
- The concealed martial world.
- Cultivation techniques, rivalries, and discoveries.
- Relationships, loyalty, disagreements, and changing responsibilities.
- The consequences of the protagonist's choices.

These stories should influence one another.

For example, an organisational decision may reveal a clue about the old man's past. A friend's personal ambition may create a new business opportunity or a conflict. A martial-world discovery may change how the organisation is perceived.

Avoid making the story feel like a collection of unrelated missions.

---

#### 12. CHOICES AND CONSEQUENCES

Player choices should matter.

Decisions should influence the world in ways that are understandable in retrospect, even when the player cannot predict every consequence.

Potential consequences include changes to:

- The four friends' trust, loyalty, opinions, and willingness to cooperate.
- Organisational finances and stability.
- Employee morale and confidence in leadership.
- The organisation's reputation among customers, suppliers, competitors, and other groups.
- Access to information, opportunities, and locations.
- The development of relationships.
- The direction and timing of investigations.
- Future alliances, conflicts, and story possibilities.
- The protagonist's public identity and influence.

Do not make every decision a simplistic good-versus-evil choice.

A decision may be financially sensible but damage trust. Another may protect a friend while creating organisational difficulties. A risky investigation might reveal important information but attract unwanted attention.

Let decisions involve competing priorities where appropriate.

##### 12.1 Consequences Must Be Coherent

Avoid arbitrary punishment.

When a decision has a negative result, the player should be able to understand the connection between their action and the outcome. Some consequences may be surprising, but they should still make sense once the relevant information becomes available.

Not every consequence needs to occur immediately. Some decisions may influence later events, opportunities, or relationships.

However, do not hide every consequence behind obscure systems. Give the player enough feedback to learn how the world responds to their choices.

##### 12.2 Friends Are People, Not Followers

The four friends must retain their own identities and agency.

They can disagree with the protagonist, question a decision, pursue personal goals, suggest alternatives, or act independently when appropriate.

They should not automatically agree with every player decision simply because the protagonist is the main character.

Their reactions should reflect their established personalities, values, ambitions, relationships, and understanding of the situation.

Disagreement should not always mean betrayal. Friends can disagree while remaining loyal, and professional conflict does not automatically destroy a personal relationship.

Likewise, high trust should not make every character obedient or erase their individuality.

---

#### 13. LONG-TERM PLAYER FREEDOM

The game should support several meaningful approaches to its world.

The player may focus on cultivation, organisational leadership, investigation, relationships, influence, or a combination of these interests.

Possible long-term paths include:

- Becoming an exceptionally capable cultivator.
- Building the organisation into a more successful and influential enterprise.
- Investigating the old man's past and the secrets of the martial world.
- Becoming a leader who coordinates powerful allies.
- Developing the four friends' capabilities and strengthening the group.
- Exploring how business, personal relationships, and cultivation affect one another.

These are directions rather than rigid classes. Do not force the player to choose one path permanently at the beginning.

The player's decisions should gradually shape their strengths, opportunities, reputation, and role in the world.

##### 13.1 The Organisation Must Remain Relevant

Do not make the organisation meaningless once the protagonist becomes powerful.

Even at high cultivation levels, the organisation should retain its own importance through people, information, finances, logistics, reputation, facilities, networks, and opportunities.

A powerful cultivator may be able to defeat an opponent personally but still need reliable employees, accurate information, good planning, or the cooperation of trusted people to solve a larger problem.

The organisation should not become a decorative menu that exists only in the early game.

Likewise, cultivation should not become irrelevant because the player is managing a business.

The two systems should create different kinds of advantages and work together where appropriate.

##### 13.2 The Four Friends Remain Important

The four friends should remain useful and interesting throughout the game, including after the protagonist reaches high cultivation levels.

Their value must not depend exclusively on being as powerful as the protagonist.

They may contribute through their own cultivation, professional competence, knowledge, judgement, relationships, leadership, investigative ability, or other strengths.

Their individual stories should continue to develop rather than ending once the protagonist becomes stronger.

Do not make them permanently inferior, helpless, or irrelevant merely to make the protagonist appear impressive.

---

#### 14. IMMERSION AND OVERALL QUALITY

The game should create the impression of a coherent world rather than a collection of disconnected mechanics.

##### 14.1 Mature, Grounded Presentation

Use a mature, visually appealing 3D style.

The game must not look childish, like a simple toy world, or like an unfinished collection of primitive shapes presented as the intended final experience.

The visual direction should feel appropriate for a modern setting that gradually reveals supernatural martial arts.

Realism is desirable where practical, but do not confuse realism with unnecessary complexity. A well-designed, stylised 3D environment can still feel mature and convincing.

##### 14.2 Compact but Meaningful Locations

Do not create a huge world merely to advertise its size.

Prefer a compact collection of useful, detailed, memorable locations that serve gameplay and storytelling.

Each location should have a reason to exist. It may support organisational work, conversations, investigation, exploration, training, combat, or important story events.

Locations should feel distinct and connected to the wider world.

Expansion should happen when it provides meaningful new experiences, not simply to increase the map's dimensions.

##### 14.3 Believable Organisation and Society

The organisation should feel like a real, established enterprise.

It should have employees, responsibilities, customers or clients, suppliers, facilities, finances, a reputation, operational problems, and opportunities for improvement.

The player should be able to make meaningful management decisions without being forced to personally perform every small task.

Delegation, planning, communication, and trust should matter.

Other people in the world should have reasons for their actions. Customers should not exist only to provide money, employees should not exist only to fill a menu, and rivals should not behave irrationally just to provoke the protagonist.

##### 14.4 Meaningful Detail

Prioritise detail that improves the experience.

Useful detail includes distinct character behaviour, readable environments, believable conversations, clear feedback, consistent rules, and consequences that follow from earlier events.

Avoid unnecessary complexity that adds work without improving the game.

The player should be able to understand the important systems without needing to memorise dozens of arbitrary rules.

---

#### 15. CREATIVE DECISIONS STILL OPEN

Do not treat every unspecified detail as a reason to stop development.

Where the design leaves room for interpretation, propose sensible options and choose a coherent solution when necessary.

However, preserve the established core requirements.

Important principles include:

- The protagonist's gender is selectable.
- The protagonist is one of five orphaned friends, all aged nineteen.
- The four friends have their own identities and agency.
- The old man transferred responsibility for the organisation six months before the present-day start.
- The old man is dead, but the friends do not know this at the beginning.
- The organisation is an established enterprise, not merely a martial arts school.
- The martial world is concealed from ordinary public understanding.
- The cultivation system follows the defined Martial, Heavenly Martial, and Divine rules.
- The protagonist is exceptionally talented but not invincible.
- The game begins in a grounded manner and gradually reveals its extraordinary elements.
- The world should be compact and meaningful rather than enormous and empty.
- The organisation must remain relevant throughout the game.
- In-game time must stop when the game is closed or inactive.

If an unresolved creative choice could substantially alter the game's identity, explain the options before committing to an irreversible direction.

Do not silently replace a core requirement with a more convenient but fundamentally different design.

---

#### 16. CREATIVE ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA

Use the following questions to evaluate whether the design is remaining faithful to the intended game.

World and Story

- Does the opening establish the protagonist's responsibilities without overwhelming the player with exposition?
- Does the present-day story begin six months after the handover?
- Does the organisation already have a functioning history and real operations?
- Is the old man's mystery revealed gradually through meaningful discoveries?
- Does the concealed martial world emerge naturally from the grounded modern setting?
- Do the personal, organisational, and martial stories connect to one another?

Characters and Relationships

- Does each of the four friends have an individual personality, ambition, and meaningful role?
- Can they disagree with the protagonist without automatically becoming enemies?
- Do their decisions and relationships develop over time?
- Do they remain relevant when the protagonist becomes powerful?
- Does the protagonist's talent leave room for other characters to matter?

Cultivation and Combat

- Are the nine Martial levels and nine Heavenly Martial levels represented consistently?
- Is Divine rank determined by the number of acquired unique Divine powers?
- Is Divine 2 the highest publicly known level at the beginning, without being treated as an absolute world limit?
- Does each additional Divine power require three times the effort of the previous acquisition?
- Does acquiring a new power provide only a modest improvement to existing powers?
- Can a lower Divine rank win through a meaningful ability matchup?
- Does combat reward tactics and understanding rather than relying entirely on rank?
- Does the protagonist's combat comprehension help without granting automatic victories?

Organisation and Freedom

- Does the organisation operate like a real enterprise?
- Can the player delegate rather than personally performing every task?
- Do management choices affect finances, people, reputation, and opportunities?
- Does the organisation continue to matter at high cultivation levels?
- Can the player pursue several different long-term interests without being locked into one narrow path?

Immersion and Time

- Does the game look mature and appealing rather than childish?
- Are locations compact, distinct, and useful?
- Does the world provide meaningful activity without requiring an enormous map?
- Does in-game time remain completely stopped while the game is closed or inactive?
- Can the player leave the game without suffering consequences caused by offline simulation?

If a proposed feature conflicts with these criteria, revise the feature or explain the trade-off before proceeding.

The overall goal is a coherent, character-driven 3D cultivation game in which ordinary responsibilities gradually connect to a much larger hidden world, the protagonist's power grows through understandable rules, the four friends remain genuine characters, and player choices meaningfully shape the journey.
